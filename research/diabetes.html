Research Focus

Diabetes Research

We study the biological overlap between insulin resistance, glucose regulation, and cardiovascular risk, working to identify early markers of Type 2 diabetes before complications set in.

Overview

Placeholder text. By the time Type 2 diabetes is formally diagnosed, insulin resistance has often been building for years. Our lab focuses on that earlier stage — the period where glucose regulation first starts to falter, long before fasting glucose or A1C levels cross a diagnostic threshold.

Because insulin resistance and vascular inflammation appear to reinforce one another, we study diabetes risk alongside cardiovascular risk rather than in isolation, looking for shared pathways that could eventually support combined screening and prevention strategies.

Current Studies

  • Multi-year study tracking glucose variability and its relationship to vascular health in at-risk adults
  • Investigation of early insulin resistance markers in patients with a family history of Type 2 diabetes
  • Pilot program evaluating continuous glucose monitoring as an early-detection tool outside of a formal diabetes diagnosis
  • Collaborative research examining shared inflammatory pathways between diabetes and cardiovascular disease

Why This Matters

Type 2 diabetes and heart disease are deeply intertwined — each raises the risk of the other, and together they account for a disproportionate share of preventable illness. Identifying risk earlier, and understanding the biology that links the two conditions, is central to changing that trajectory.

This research is funded in part by individual donors.
