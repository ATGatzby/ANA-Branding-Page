Research Focus

Heart Disease Research

Our lab investigates the earliest biological changes that precede clinical heart disease, with a focus on how chronic vascular inflammation and metabolic dysfunction interact to accelerate cardiovascular risk long before symptoms appear.

Overview

Placeholder text. Heart disease rarely begins with a single dramatic event — it develops gradually, through years of low-grade inflammation, endothelial stress, and metabolic strain on the vascular system. Our work focuses on mapping that early window, using a combination of clinical cohort data and laboratory models to understand which biological signals show up first, and which of those signals can realistically be used to flag risk earlier than current screening allows.

We pay particular attention to the overlap between vascular inflammation and metabolic dysfunction, since the two appear to accelerate each other in ways that standard cardiovascular risk models don't fully capture.

Current Studies

  • Longitudinal cohort study tracking vascular inflammation markers in adults with early metabolic syndrome
  • Investigation of endothelial dysfunction as an early predictor of coronary artery disease
  • Evaluation of a novel anti-inflammatory approach to reducing cardiovascular risk in prediabetic patients
  • Collaborative imaging study using advanced MRI techniques to visualize arterial plaque formation over time

Why This Matters

Heart disease remains the leading cause of death worldwide, and much of that burden is preventable if risk is caught early enough. By understanding the biological signals that precede clinical disease, our goal is to open a wider window for earlier, more effective intervention — before a first cardiac event, not after.

This research is funded in part by individual donors.
